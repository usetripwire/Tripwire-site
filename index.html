The narrative

The trenches got rigged. So we strung wire.

Every new coin looks the same on a chart. Behind half of them sits a dev who bundled a third of the supply across fresh wallets, snipers who landed in block zero, and a wall of volume that is just the same SOL going in circles.

Then the bots arrived. AI agent wallets went from roughly 8% to 34% of daily Solana memecoin DEX volume between March and June 2026, trading several times as often as humans. Often you are not reading the crowd. You are reading machines reading each other.

And when it goes wrong, it goes wrong fast. The dev sells, the bundle unwinds, the liquidity disappears, and by the time the candle prints, the exit is gone.

Soldiers strung tripwires so nobody crept up in the dark. Tripwire does the same for the memecoin trenches: it checks the ground before you step in, and keeps watch while you're there.

The full scan

Six wires. One read.

Every scan rebuilds the launch from on-chain history: who bought first, who funded them, what the dev did before, and who is trading now. Each wire stays clear or trips, with the reason spelled out. Authority and holder checks run on every scan too.

Full scan

Agent wire

Full scan

Separates agent wallets from human ones by trade rhythm and fingerprint, so you know how much of the buying pressure is people.

Wash wire

Full scan

Finds volume that loops between related wallets and strips it out, so the number you see is volume that actually changed hands.

Six watch wires

Dev move

The deployer or a wallet it funded sells, or moves tokens to a new wallet or an exchange.

Bundle unwind

Wallets in the launch bundle start selling together, even when each sale is small.

Liquidity pull

LP is removed, unlocked, or drops sharply within minutes.

Sniper exit

The block-zero snipers sell a large share of what they still hold.

Whale dump

A top holder sells a large part of their position in one go.

Authority change

Mint, freeze, metadata or transfer-fee settings on the token change.
